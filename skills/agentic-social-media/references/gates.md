# Mandatory evidence, discoverability and editorial gates

## Hard gates H1–H6 (any material failure = HOLD or SKIP)
H1 Truth: supported facts; results show methods/baselines; hypotheses labeled.
H2 Confidentiality: no unauthorized employer/customer/IP/private details.
H3 Reader value: useful evidence, method, decision or defensible new argument.
H4 Authenticity: no false experience, engagement bait, manipulated reaction strategy or copied posts.
H5 Audience: identified reader and correct platform relevance.
H6 Integrity: truthful attribution; no misleading source, fake chart, tag or endorsement.

## A–C: discoverability and relationship
A — Taxonomy and reader job: one primary pillar, one concrete reader and useful takeaway, verified technical entities, independent platform fit. REVISE or SKIP if not.
B — Keywords and hashtags: natural language first. Public hashtags default NONE; LinkedIn optionally 1–3 for a specific topical/community purpose, not claimed distribution benefit; X max 2 if useful; blogs use real categories/search queries, not hashtags. REMOVE generic/trending tags lacking rationale.
C — Mentions: verify identity and profile URL; record real contribution, source credit, or event role and potential consent. Do not imply sponsorship. Remove unrelated tags; HOLD collaborator coauthorship without agreement. Blog uses attribution links, not @mentions.

## D–G: reading and voice
D — First two lines demonstrate exactly why a reader cares, without delaying the reveal.
E — Central insight in opening third; follow promptly with evidence, remove fluff.
F — If firsthand claim, real artifact/metric/demo; if research, dated primary source and counterargument; if visual, no false implied evidence. HOLD unverifiable central claim.
G — Natural human prose: direct, precise, varied paragraphs; delete formulaic suspense, empty inspirational endings, excessive em dashes, unrelated hashtags, emoji chains, invented first-person reactions and generic CTAs.

## Scores (editorial heuristic, never an algorithm prediction)
0–5 each: originality/value, hook, early payoff, evidence, natural voice, audience fit, mobile-friendly format, meaningful takeaway. Default READY >=34/40 and evidence/voice >=4 *after* all hard gates pass. Expert disagreement, privacy and factual truth override points.

## Adversarial check
What would an experienced engineer object to? Does the first paragraph stall? Has a benchmark actually run? Is a mentioned person truly relevant? Does the visual imply proof that doesn't exist? What concrete value remains if author's identity is hidden?
