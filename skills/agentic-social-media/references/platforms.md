# Channel-native content decisions

Decide independently, with a primary reader, single claim, supporting proof and counterargument per channel. Platform verdicts PUBLISH / ADAPT / HOLD / SKIP are editorial routing only; publishing requires explicit approval.

## LinkedIn — primary
Use for a real engineering/product decision, real event/contribution, meaningful trade-off, documented project insight or defensible market argument. First two lines should provide the reader's reason to care; deliver main result early, then one or two concrete details and a grounded takeaway. Test 80–140 words as an editorial constraint, not a ranking formula. More words only when useful. Avoid boilerplate intros, clickbait questions and unnatural one-line cadence. Prefer real screenshots/demos or reproducible artifacts. Natural technical search terms; zero hashtags by default, up to three only with clear topical utility. Tag genuine collaborators with verified identity and consent, not brands for exposure. SKIP repetitive hype or unsupported geopolitical forecasts.

## X — secondary
One precise, independently valuable observation by default, especially when timely or addressing a relevant technical discussion. Thread only if every unit adds information. Avoid compressed LinkedIn copy, performative hot takes, mass replies, hashtag storms or tagging people without substantive reason. Zero hashtags by default, at most two if useful; verify exact handles. HOLD if nuance cannot survive short form or sources remain uncertain.

## Personal blog — tertiary
Durable implementation walkthrough, technical comparison, methodology, reproducible experiment or substantial research. Title and opening answer a real search question; then evidence, how it works, alternatives, limitations, reproducibility, links and accessibility. Use precise slug/title/H1/canonical metadata where appropriate, not social hashtags. No forced length target or keyword stuffing. HOLD rather than expanding a short opinion into a hollow article.

## Platform fit example
A sourced CUDA portability framework with a rigorous migration checklist may suit a blog but not a second abstract LinkedIn post until the author has new measured proof. A real storefront screenshot might suit LinkedIn immediately while the blog waits for a full architecture walkthrough. The system should say SKIP or HOLD rather than fill all three slots.
