# Skill: LinkedIn (PRIMARY)

**Job:** build authentic technical, product and founder credibility among engineers, tech leads and builders. Not a résumé broadcast or AI-news feed.

## Fit gate
PUBLISH when there is one specific professional takeaway plus credible proof or strong sourced argument. ADAPT when useful but too speculative/generic. SKIP when personal trivia, repetitive news or unsupported grand claim.
Most useful forms: first-person demonstration with result; hard engineering trade-off; counterintuitive product observation with artifact; team project with verifiable credit; backed market thesis with explicit limitation.

## Composition
- First 1–2 lines: exact claim/problem for a clear reader. Don't hide the payoff after 'see more'.
- Next: concrete proof, artifact, choice, number or example. Main insight no later than first third.
- Last: implication. Optional narrow question only when authentic and answerable.
- Default **80–140 words as an experiment**; expand only for meaningful proof or nuance. Shorter isn't intrinsically algorithmically superior.
- Normal readable paragraphs. Don't convert each sentence to a single line. No gimmicky 'viral' typography.
- Prefer a real screenshot, demo, code/result crop or useful clean diagram over decorative infographics.
- Tags: only real collaborators and source organizations, when relevant. Hashtags: 0–3 high-relevance; no reach promises. Links can appear where useful; don't invent penalties.
- Consider privacy: avoid language signaling job search to employer; no internal system details without clearance.
- For collaborative work: accurately distinguish team result from personal contribution. Confirm co-authoring/mentions feature availability before advising exact UI.
- Never guarantee impressions or likes; performance evaluated with comparable windows and relevant readership.

## Review
What does this teach an engineer or founder within 5 seconds? Could the post exist without this author's actual work? If yes, consider HOLD until proof is added.
